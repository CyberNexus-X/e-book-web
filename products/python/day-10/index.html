Day 10: for Loop and range()

Phase: Beginner · Reels: 46/450 – 50/450

Quick Answer

Day 10 teaches the for loop and range(). range(5) gives 0, 1, 2, 3, 4: the end value is never included.

What You Will Learn

  • range(5) produces 0 to 4. The number 5 itself is not included.
  • range(2, 11, 2) starts at 2, stops before 11, and steps by 2: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.
  • sum(range(1, 11)) adds 1 to 10 and gives 55.
  • enumerate() gives both a number and an item while looping over a list.
  • Use for when you know what to loop over or how many times to loop.

Code

for i in range(5):
    print(i, end=" ")
print()
print(list(range(2, 11, 2)))
print(sum(range(1, 11)))
for i, f in enumerate(["aam", "kela"], start=1):
    print(i, f)
for i in range(1, 4):
    print(f"5 x {i} = {5 * i}")

Output

0 1 2 3 4 
[2, 4, 6, 8, 10]
55
1 aam
2 kela
5 x 1 = 5
5 x 2 = 10
5 x 3 = 15

Code Explained, Line by Line

  1. range(5) produces 0 to 4. The number 5 itself is not included.
  2. range(2, 11, 2) starts at 2, stops before 11, and steps by 2: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.
  3. sum(range(1, 11)) adds 1 to 10 and gives 55.
  4. enumerate() gives both a number and an item while looping over a list.
  5. Use for when you know what to loop over or how many times to loop.
Common Mistake: To get 1 to 10 you need range(1, 11), not range(1, 10), because the end value is never included.

Real-Life Use

Tables, lists of items and repeated calculations in apps are all for loops.
